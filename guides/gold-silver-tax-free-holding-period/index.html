Physical Gold & Silver Taxes: How the 1-Year Holding Period and FIFO Work

When it comes to physical precious metals, time is just as valuable as purity. In several jurisdictions, holding bullion for over 365 days turns a taxable gain into a 100% tax-free return | provided you can prove the exact purchase date.

2026-09-06 · MyGoldFolio

Holding physical gold and silver for over one year unlocks substantial tax advantages in many jurisdictions, including complete exemption under Germany’s § 23 EStG. Master the difference between a threshold and an allowance, apply FIFO lot tracking to sell your oldest lots first, and keep verifiable invoice dates to secure your tax-free gains.

The overlooked advantage: why holding duration matters

Unlike stocks or interest-bearing paper assets that trigger automatic withholding taxes, physical precious metals are treated as tangible personal property in many jurisdictions. This creates a distinct legal boundary between short-term speculative trading and long-term private wealth preservation.

The core principle is simple: governments often reward long-term custody by reducing or entirely eliminating capital gains tax once an asset passes a defined holding threshold. But capturing this benefit requires clear documentation and disciplined calendar tracking.

  • Physical bullion is classified as tangible personal property, not financial securities.
  • Holding past a statutory timeline can legally reduce capital gains tax to zero.
  • The tax clock starts on the exact transaction date shown on your purchase invoice.

The 1-year holding rule explained

In countries like Germany (§ 23 Abs. 1 Nr. 2 EStG) and Austria, private sales of physical gold, silver, platinum, and palladium follow a strict 12-month rule:

If you hold the physical metal for more than 365 days before selling, any realized profit is completely tax-free | regardless of how much the price increased. You do not even need to declare the sale on your annual tax return.

If you sell within 12 months, the profit is taxed as other income at your personal income tax rate. However, an annual exemption limit (Freigrenze) applies: up to €1,000 in total private sales profits per calendar year remains tax-free.

  • Over 12 months: 100% tax-free profit with zero reporting requirement.
  • Under 12 months: Taxed at personal income tax rate if annual gains reach €1,000.
  • Crucial: €1,000 is an all-or-nothing threshold (Freigrenze), not an allowance (Freibetrag). If your profit is €1,001, the entire €1,001 is taxable.

Physical bullion vs paper certificates

A vital distinction exists between allocated physical bullion and paper financial products (such as standard gold ETFs, ETCs, or mining stocks).

Standard financial derivatives are generally subject to flat investment withholding taxes (such as Germany’s 25% Abgeltungsteuer plus solidarity surcharge), regardless of how many years you hold them. Only physical bars, coins, and specific physically deliverable certificates (such as Xetra-Gold under German Federal Fiscal Court rulings) qualify for personal property tax exemptions.

FIFO: why the order of purchase matters

If you accumulate gold or silver gradually over several years, you will own multiple purchase lots bought at different dates and prices. When you decide to sell a portion of your holdings, how do tax authorities know which lot you sold?

Tax frameworks universally apply the First-In, First-Out (FIFO) method for identical fungible assets: the earliest purchased lots are legally considered the first ones sold.

  • FIFO automatically matches your sale against your oldest recorded purchase.
  • This ensures you sell tax-matured lots (> 1 year) first, keeping your capital gains tax at zero.
  • Your newer, still-maturing purchases remain in the portfolio to continue their countdown undisturbed.

The proof trap: no receipt = no tax exemption

The legal burden of proof (Nachweispflicht) rests entirely on you as the taxpayer. If you sell gold and claim the 1-year tax exemption, the tax office can request proof of when you acquired it and how much you paid.

If you cannot produce an invoice or written transaction record showing the acquisition date, tax authorities can disallow your tax-free status or assess tax on the full gross sale proceeds.

  • Always store original dealer invoices with date, weight, and price.
  • For private purchases, insist on a signed bill of sale with date and signatures.
  • Keep transaction records for at least several years after selling.

Frequently asked questions

Does the 1-year tax-free rule apply to physical silver as well as gold?

In Germany under § 23 EStG, the 1-year holding exemption applies to all physical precious metals (Gold, Silver, Platinum, and Palladium) held as private assets. However, remember that Silver was subject to VAT upon purchase, which becomes part of your recorded cost basis.

What happens if I sell gold after 11 months and 25 days?

If you sell even a single day before the full 12-month period expires, the transaction is treated as a short-term sale. If your total annual profit from private sales exceeds the €1,000 threshold, the entire profit is taxable at your regular income tax rate.

How do I prove the purchase date for gold bought years ago?

Dealers issue invoices with date, purity, and product description. For private purchases, a written bill of sale with the seller’s confirmation serves as proof. Keep these records permanently alongside your holding documentation.
