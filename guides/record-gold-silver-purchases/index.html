Track Gold and Silver: 7 details to record for every purchase

Clear portfolio tracking starts at the moment you record a purchase. A small, consistent set of details is usually more useful than a long list you will never maintain.

2026-08-15 · MyGoldFolio

You do not need to turn every coin or bar into paperwork. Record the same seven essentials for each purchase and you can understand the position later|without guessing.

Why a purchase record matters

The portfolio total is useful, but it cannot explain where it came from. Individual records make it easier to compare purchases made at different times, understand costs, and revisit a possible sale without reconstructing old invoices.

The goal is not perfect administration. It is a record that still makes sense months or years later.

The seven details worth recording

Use the same fields for every Gold or Silver purchase. If a detail is unavailable, leave a short note rather than inventing precision.

DetailWhat to noteWhy it helps later
Metal and formGold or Silver; coin, bar, jewellery, or another physical item.A clear description keeps similar purchases distinguishable.
Quantity and weightNumber of items plus gross weight where useful.You can still identify the physical lot behind the record.
Purity and fine weightPurity and the fine-metal weight when known.Fine weight gives a more comparable basis across products.
Purchase dateThe date you bought it.It preserves the order of purchases and your own history.
Total costThe amount actually paid, including premium, delivery, and other fees.A purchase price alone can understate what the position cost you.
CurrencyThe currency used for the purchase.It keeps an original amount understandable when your display currency changes.
Receipt referenceA neutral invoice number, file name, or short note.You can find supporting records without putting sensitive details into the portfolio.

Keep the record practical

Consistency matters more than detail. Choose one weight convention, describe products in a way you will recognise, and use the same approach for every purchase.

  • Enter the purchase soon after it happens, while the receipt is available.
  • Use the paid total rather than trying to remember premiums or delivery later.
  • Keep optional notes short and useful: for example, a product name or receipt reference.
  • Record a sale against the relevant holding when it happens instead of replacing its original purchase details.

Be careful with storage details

A portfolio record can help you organise holdings, but it should not become a map to them. Avoid exact home addresses, safe combinations, account numbers, or other sensitive access details in a spreadsheet, an export, or a note you may share.

A neutral reminder such as “private storage” or “document filed separately” is normally enough. Keep receipts and backups in places you control.

Start with what you already know

You do not need to complete a historic portfolio in one sitting. Start with the clearest recent purchase, establish your convention, then add older holdings as you find the information.

  1. Record one purchase with the seven essentials.
  2. Check whether you could understand it again without opening the invoice.
  3. Use the same pattern for the next purchase or holding.

A clear record supports a clear view

The best record is the one you will keep. With a small, repeatable set of details, a spreadsheet or tracking app can show more than one total: it can keep each purchase understandable.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need every detail for every old purchase?

No. Start with what you can verify: metal, weight, purchase date, and total cost. Add purity or a receipt reference when you have it; consistent records are more useful than guessed details.

Should I record gross weight or fine weight?

Both can be useful. Gross weight identifies the physical item; fine weight makes products with different purity easier to compare. Record fine weight when it is known from the product or its documentation.

Is it safe to record where I store Gold and Silver?

Use only a neutral note if it helps you organise records. Do not store exact locations, access details, account numbers, or other sensitive information in a portfolio file or export.
